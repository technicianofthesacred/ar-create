# The carousel guide

Every carousel AR Create makes follows this guide, whether it is cut from an Article or made in the Instagram studio from a voice note and photos. Agents read it before they write a carousel. Adrian reads it to know what they were asked to do. Check runs the scroll test at the end against the same rules.

It adapts the 35 checks from "Claude + Carousel Maxxing" (Grow with Alex, 2026, youtu.be/QSNU1_71nLo), adopted by Adrian on 27 November 2026. Where the video and Adrian's own rules disagree, his rules win, and each place is named below.

What a carousel is for here: a reader who is on a similar path saves it because it is useful, sends it to a friend because it says something true, and swipes to the end because every slide gives them something new. Likes are not the measure. Depth, not dopamine.

## 1. The cover

The cover gets about one second in the feed. Its job is to make someone stop, not to explain.

1. **Big, short, readable.** About 12 words or fewer. People glance at a cover; they do not read it.
2. **Say what the reader gets, in plain words.** Not just the topic ("tea storage") but what they walk away with ("Why the jar matters more than the tea"). Never bait: no "steal my", no "you won't believe", no promise the carousel does not keep. (Adrian's rule over the video's.)
3. **Be specific.** A real number, a real name, a real place. Specific reads as true; vague reads as opinion.
4. **One thing to look at.** One photograph, or one line, where the eye lands first.
5. **Look different from the feed.** Real photographs of the real thing stand out against stock and templates.
6. **Readable on a phone.** It will be seen at the size of a stamp. If it only works on a big screen, it does not work.
7. **Leave a reason to swipe.** A cover that feels complete gets a glance and a scroll. Open a question; do not list every answer on it.

## 2. The swipe

Every slide has one job: earn the next swipe.

8. **Slide 2 keeps the cover's promise, at once.** Instagram can show a carousel again starting from slide 2, so slide 2 is a second cover. Start delivering on it here. For Teajia stories the Article still holds the whole story; the carousel delivers its own takeaway and the Article goes deeper. (This replaces the older "hold the payoff back" line: begin the payoff early, keep the depth for the Article.)
9. **One idea per slide.** People swipe in under two seconds. Two ideas on one slide and they leave with neither.
10. **Short lines, big text.** About 8 words or fewer on a photograph slide, about 15 to 35 on a teaching slide. These are aims for a phone, never a reason to clip a quote, a caveat or the meaning.
11. **Every slide earns the next.** Nobody decides to read the whole carousel; they decide one swipe at a time. Each middle slide adds an example, a fact, a voice or a turn. None repeats the slide before it.
12. **Show where they are.** People finish what they can see the end of. Use the layouts' slide numbers where they have them.
13. **Cut the filler.** Every slide that adds nothing is a place to leave. Use the fewest slides that carry the hook, the turn and the takeaway: usually 4 to 10.
14. **End strong.** The last slide gives the real takeaway and at most one next step, usually the Article. A carousel from a voice note may end on its last true line with no ask at all. No "like, comment, share and follow", no "comment one word". (Adrian's rule over the video's.)

## 3. The visuals

Slides should look like the real thing, not like slides.

15. **Show the real thing.** The actual tea, cup, place or person beats a picture about it.
16. **Real photographs, never stock, never invented.** Use only photographs that exist in the story's folder or the Library. Never describe a picture that may not exist. No AI-made backgrounds and no paid image services. (Adrian's rule over the video's.)
17. **Make the point the biggest thing.** The most important line or picture is the largest thing on the slide.
18. **Keep one look.** The same look and layout family across the whole carousel, so it reads as one post from one account.
19. **Leave space.** Empty space makes a slide easier to read.
20. **Zoom in before it is ready.** Look at every slide up close: soft photographs, typos, a crop that cuts a face, words running out of their box.
21. **Recognisable at a glance.** People should know it is Adrian's (or Teajia's) before they read the name. The look does this, not a logo.

## 4. The system

22. **Words before design.** The hook, the payoff, the proof, the next step, all written and reviewed first. Layouts and photographs come after, and never change the reviewed words.
23. **Never from a blank page.** Start from what is already there: the Article, the brief, the voice note, the saved ideas.
24. **Learn from what worked.** When Adrian says a carousel did well, note what it did, and do more of that.

(The video's points on batching, templates, a swipe file and a posting schedule are how Adrian works, not rules for each carousel, so they are not checked here.)

## 5. The reach

25. **Worth saving.** Something the reader will come back to: a practice, a distinction, a way to tell one thing from another.
26. **Worth sending.** Something true enough that a reader wants a friend to see it.
27. **The caption's first line is a second hook.** It shows under the post before "more", so make it count in about 125 characters or fewer. Then two to four short sentences.

(Posting times, replying to comments and reposting winners happen outside AR Create, which never posts, so they are not checked here.)

## The scroll test

Check reads the saved carousel the way a scrolling reader meets it, and shows:

- **The cover**: how many of its countable checks it passes (short enough, one thought, not repeated later).
- **Every slide**: "swipe" (a reason to keep going) or "leave" (a reason to stop), with the reason in one line: too many words for its kind, repeats an earlier slide, empty.
- **Slides to cut**: the ones that repeat or carry nothing.
- **The biggest fix**: the one change to make first.
- **For your eye**: what no count can tell (does the cover stand out, does slide 2 keep the promise, does each photograph show the real thing).

The scroll test is advice. It never blocks a carousel, never changes a word, and never stands in for Adrian's yes.

**Agents**, before you hand a carousel over, read it once as a scrolling reader, not as a designer. Be blunt with yourself:

- Cover: does it blend into the feed? Where does the eye land first? Can you say what it is about in six words? Is there a reason to swipe?
- Every slide: one line on whether it gives a reason to swipe or a reason to leave.
- Verdict: which slides to cut, and the single biggest fix.

Fix what you can within the rules above, then report the rest in a line or two beside the carousel.

## How to get better without trying everything at once

Do not try to meet all of these on every carousel. Look at the last few, pick the two or three checks they miss most, and watch only those for a week. Then the next few.
