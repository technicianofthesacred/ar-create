# Make AR Create understandable when the human returns

Adopted 13 October 2026. These rules govern user-facing chats, interface text and result presentation. Technical code, developer documentation and saved evidence may retain precise engineering vocabulary. This is an experience requirement, not proof that every screen already meets it.

## Write for someone arriving without the history

Adrian often opens a chat or screen without the preceding context. Make its purpose, current work and next useful action understandable from what is visible. Use a compact introduction on entry or a meaningful handoff; do not repeat headings or instructions on every message.

Name things by what the person can do or understand. Prefer “Use my existing folders,” “Find my stories and latest edits,” “Edit articles and carousels,” and “Use my magazine workflow.” Explain files as files, edits as edits and decisions as decisions. Keep implementation vocabulary, IDs, hashes, commands and proof-file lists in developer details unless they help resolve the user's actual question.

Use **AR Create** for the whole workspace in user-facing explanations. “Document Study” is a historical prototype/directory name and may remain in code and existing URLs. A story/document opens within the workspace; do not describe the whole application as only a document viewer. Preserve routes and technical identifiers unless their change is separately authorized.

## Give the person a useful entry point

A human-facing Build update includes the actual preview/result link, what is available to try, the relevant change, known limits and a plain-language feedback path. Show the working interface or rendered comparison; prose/code descriptions do not establish visual success. Ask for a concrete judgment, not a vague approval of everything.

The person can point at appearance, spacing, behavior, missing tools or wording in ordinary language. Receive that feedback in the Build/story/Design/Use chat where the visual context is available. Preserve the distinction between “looks right,” “works,” “preserves my edits,” and “approved for publication.”

## Let the work connect without making the person relay it

Start Here is an optional dependable entry point. Deep Review and Build can receive direct human decisions. Record adopted intent once in the relevant brief; send Delivery only the actionable change and reference needed for coordination. Keep recommendations visibly separate from chosen direction. The person does not repeat the discussion in Start Here or Delivery.

Delivery manages implementation ownership, dependencies, finishing conditions and verified results. Internal connection/repository chats serve engineering work; they are not a checklist Adrian must operate. Review means a result needs human judgment; reuse its existing working conversation unless a sustained new conversation is justified. Saved work means earlier development is retained; it is not an instruction to the person or evidence that the feature is installed.

Keep task completion, human acceptance, chat unread state and chat archival separate. Preserve all continuing topics and artifacts, then ask Adrian before archiving a chat. Keep one existing delivery record and a compact current-state pointer; avoid repeated status copies, recursive acknowledgments and new agents simply to keep chats busy. Use the maintained project-collaboration playbook for the detailed operating process.

## Design the interface around the actual activity

A stage should show the relevant material and the tools needed to work with it. People should see their sources, words, photos and progress rather than encounter generic record placeholders or agent command instructions. Distinguish a recording, its transcripts/corrections, research, authored wording and requested output; connect them visibly without inventing missing material or progress.

Keep the document-first foundation, readable sans-serif text, project navigation, compact useful controls, direct editing and appropriate margin discussion. Preserve each surface's purpose rather than imposing one layout everywhere. Put paths, provenance and technical details behind clear secondary disclosure. Avoid unnecessary top headers, repeated information and horizontal scrolling.

The person must be able to find and resume a story, identify the selected output and see what needs attention. Use human descriptions for empty, processing, failed, read-only and changed-since-review states. Explain what can be done next; do not imply AI ran, sources were verified, work was saved or an output was published without the corresponding evidence.

## Check changes through use

Before a changed screen is called usable, open the actual view and ask: Can a returning person tell what this is, what they are looking at, what they can do, and what is still unfinished? Verify the affected action, not just labels, along with required save/switch/recovery behavior. For visual changes inspect the rendered applicable themes and widths; do not ask the person to mentally render code.

When feedback reveals a reusable problem, update these rules or the shared collaboration playbook and relevant brief; preserve their distinct responsibilities rather than duplicating all rules everywhere. Read newer direct human instructions before implementing. This request records rules; it does not authorize an unbounded redesign or claim existing screens have been repaired.
