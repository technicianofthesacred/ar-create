Back end: new files only (a service, a commands file, tests) plus the registry lines in runtime/commands.mjs, the server route line, the CLI group, and docs/START.md's data map row.

What the owner decided: a research topic is separate from a subject, so one topic can feed many subjects and research is for learning, not only for preparing an article. Topics mostly make themselves (from a conversation, a fact check, things he saved, his note) and he can also start one. Topics can be clumped into groups, like a notebook holding many sources, which he can ask questions of. It must stay quiet: read first, decide one thing at a time, nothing forced.

Today research belongs to one subject (runtime/services/research-material.mjs, research-answer.mjs, study-research-*.mjs; commands `research-material.*`, `research.answers.*`, `study.research.*`; the read-only cross-subject view public/document-study/research-tab.js). Do NOT build a second research engine or copy findings. Build a thin layer of records on top of what exists:
1. Records, under the home's data in `research-topics/`, through the store helpers with actor stamp, journal and revision: a topic (id, plain name, where it came from: conversation, fact check, saved things, note, or started by him, with a pointer to that origin; the subjects it is used in; pointers to the existing research material, findings and answers that belong to it; pointers to Library sources; its group; state such as new, has something to say, let go) and a group (id, name, folder path, the Library sources ticked for asking, its topics, how it works: plain settings). Pointers, never copies.
2. Commands in the registry, assistants allowed (filing and proposing; his own notes, positions and decisions stay his alone as in the existing research commands): list topics with filters (group, has something to say, made this week), read a topic with what it points to resolved through the existing research commands, save a topic, let one go and undo, link a topic to a subject, groups list, read and save, add and remove a group's sources, and "propose a topic" for assistants (a proposal he can keep or let go, reusing the app's proposal pattern). A read that gathers, for a subject, the topics it uses.
3. Bringing existing research in without a migration: a subject's existing research shows up as one topic for that subject the first time it is read (made on the fly from what is there, saved only when something about it is changed).
4. Asking a group or "across everything": find what the app can already answer from kept material (research.answers, the Atlas search, Library search). Wire the ask box to the real one that fits, show its answer with the sources it names, and say plainly what it cannot answer. If nothing in the app can answer across sources without an AI provider, the ask box is shown as "not built yet" with one line saying what it needs. Never a canned answer.
5. Screens (mock-up #research, #group, #topic, third pass onward): the research home with a slim rail of groups that folds into one control on narrow screens (no sideways scrolling), "Made for you this week" saying where each came from with Let it go and a suggested group, then topics by group; a group page (sources with ticks, the ask, topics inside, used in, how this group works); a topic page with three tabs: Read (one calm page: what it is, what people say with who agrees and who disputes drawn from the findings, spoken words in Lora, and his one line of what he takes from it, which only he writes), Decide (one waiting thing at a time, each can be left), Sources. New topic.
6. Tests for the service and commands (revision conflict, undo, pointers resolve, a subject's old research appears as a topic, an assistant cannot write his own notes), and page tests for the screen's hooks.
