PIECE 8, WHERE A SUBJECT STANDS. Read /tmp/briefs/v2-common.md first and follow it (you may skim the mock-up: look at #home and #subject and the pictures in /tmp/shots-v2/home/ and /tmp/shots-v2/subject/). Worktree: /tmp/wt-v2-d (branch claude/v2-d, reset to the build branch head). Demo port: 8858. Back end only: no file under public/v2/screens/ and nothing under public/document-study/.

Why: version two's Home and Subject screens each work out, in the browser, where a subject stands (its station, its ring, its next step; and for one subject, every step's state per form). To do that they call many existing reads, some of which are slow once there are many subjects: `inquiries.projects` takes 4 to 6 seconds at 24 subjects and about 90 seconds at 150 while holding the server; `study.research.brief` takes 1.4 to 3.2 seconds; Home makes about 185 calls at 150 subjects and settles after 28 seconds; the Subject page shows a wrong step for five seconds and then jumps. The two screens also disagree with each other and with the records in places (listed below). The owner has one to two hundred subjects.

Build one truthful, fast source for both screens:
1. One pure module that decides where a subject stands from plain gathered records, with no reading inside it: for one subject and one form, the list of steps (group Gather, Research, Simmer, Create or Share; id; plain name; state done, next, needs a look, to do, or waiting; what a waiting step waits for; a short note; the section id of the existing workspace it belongs to) and from those the station (Gather, Research, Create, Share, Published or Resting), the ring fraction and the next step in plain words. It must run unchanged on the server and in the browser: put it where the build already makes browser copies of shared modules (see runtime/scripts/build-document-study.mjs and how ig-codes reaches the browser) and have the build emit a copy version two can load; say in your report the path version two should load and the names it exposes.
   Start from the two mappings that exist, so the wording and the step lists the owner has seen do not change: the Subject screen's in /tmp/wt-v2-c/public/v2/screens/subject.js (its pure function and tests/v2-subject.test.mjs) and Home's in /tmp/wt-d-subjects/public/v2/screens/home.js (tests/v2-home.test.mjs). Both files are being edited by their builders right now; read them, do not change them. Where they disagree, the Subject screen's step logic wins and Home's station is derived from it.
   Make it right in the cases a reviewer found wrong: a finished Article has The words done and Check next; while a suggestion waits on the words, The words is the step that needs him and Check and Publish wait; "photographs in the article" waits for photographs being added, not for "What you have"; "Name the dish" is done only when the records really hold the named dish (find where the Simmer board keeps it and where his direction is kept, and decide truthfully); a subject that holds a voice note and notes is not "nothing yet"; a subject with an Article and a chosen Carousel shows the next step of its first unfinished form; a Carousel-only subject still reaches Write, Read and Check from a real step; every one of the workspace's twelve sections is reachable from some step. Tests for each, and for each of the seven kinds of subject the demo fill makes (scripts/demo-fill.mjs).
2. One gathered read for one subject (suggest `subject.standing`): everything the module needs for that subject and its forms, read on the server in one call, cheaply, without calling any read that lists every subject. Find, for each fact, the cheapest place it is kept (read the services, not only their commands).
3. One summary read for all subjects (suggest `subjects.standing`): for every subject its station, ring, next step, forms with their rings, when it last changed, and its look if a plan names one; one call for Home. It must be fast at size: under 300 ms warm at 200 subjects and under 2 seconds cold, never holding the server (yield while working), and kept fresh by the journal or the change stream rather than recomputed on every call (a small cache keyed by what changed; say how it is invalidated and prove with a test that a change to a subject shows up in the next read). Measure on the demo with `npm run demo:fill -- --large` and on a synthetic 2000.
4. Registry commands (read-only, any actor), CLI verbs, MCP reach, the data map row if you keep a cache file (prefer memory), and tests.
5. While you are in those services: note, do not fix, any existing read that holds the server for more than a second at 150 subjects, with its measured time, so the owner can be told.

Report in under 250 words: the path and names for the browser, the two commands and their measured times at 150 and 2000, how freshness works, which facts you could not find cheaply, the slow existing reads you measured, and test totals.
