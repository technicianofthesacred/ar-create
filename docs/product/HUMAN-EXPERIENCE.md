# Make AR Create understandable when the human returns

Current typography authority: [PRODUCT: Typography](PRODUCT.md#typography) and final C Workbench NOTES supersede the older broad Atlas assignments below. Preserve the later Source-specific Atlas treatment; Research, notes and Write/Preview retain their current C roles. Historical decisions below remain recorded without granting a global font replacement.

Adopted 13 October 2026. These rules govern user-facing chats, interface text and result presentation. Technical code, developer documentation and saved evidence may retain precise engineering vocabulary. This is an experience requirement, not proof that every screen already meets it.

## Write for someone arriving without the history

Adrian often opens a chat or screen without the preceding context. Make its purpose, current work and next useful action understandable from what is visible. Use a compact introduction on entry or a meaningful handoff; do not repeat headings or instructions on every message.

Name things by what the person can do or understand. Prefer “Use my existing folders,” “Find my stories and latest edits,” “Edit articles and carousels,” and “Use my magazine workflow.” Explain files as files, edits as edits and decisions as decisions. Keep implementation vocabulary, IDs, hashes, commands and proof-file lists in developer details unless they help resolve the user's actual question.

Use **AR Create** for the whole workspace in user-facing explanations. “Document Study” is a historical prototype/directory name and may remain in code and existing URLs. A story/document opens within the workspace; do not describe the whole application as only a document viewer. Preserve routes and technical identifiers unless their change is separately authorized.

## Give the person a useful entry point

A human-facing Build update includes the actual preview/result link, what is available to try, the relevant change, known limits and a plain-language feedback path. Show the working interface or rendered comparison; prose/code descriptions do not establish visual success. Ask for a concrete judgment, not a vague approval of everything.

The person can point at appearance, spacing, behavior, missing tools or wording in ordinary language. Receive that feedback in the Build/story/Design/Use chat where the visual context is available. Preserve the distinction between “looks right,” “works,” “preserves my edits,” and “approved for publication.”

## Only call a chat Review when there is something ready to review

A human-facing Review chat must present a readable or visual result that can actually be opened, with its current version, the specific feedback wanted and relevant unfinished parts. Code snippets, Markdown layout instructions, JSON completion files and technical handoffs are builder records; they do not qualify as a human preview.

Keep a draft or earlier development chat under Saved work while its preview is missing, broken or not yet prepared. Preserve its artifacts and history. Delivery's human-review queue uses the same gate: a saved result with no usable preview belongs in preparation or saved work, not in the person's action list. Reuse the existing working chat when the result becomes ready; no new permanent window is required just because a task changes state.

Before changing a chat to Review, its owner opens the result, confirms that it displays the actual intended work, and posts the visible link and a short plain-language feedback request. A file existing or a technical completion marker is insufficient. Renaming does not change the artifact, mean the person has approved it or permit archival.

## Let the work connect without making the person relay it

Start Here is an optional dependable entry point. Deep Review and Build can receive direct human decisions. Record adopted intent once in the relevant brief; send Delivery only the actionable change and reference needed for coordination. Keep recommendations visibly separate from chosen direction. The person does not repeat the discussion in Start Here or Delivery.

Delivery manages implementation ownership, dependencies, finishing conditions and verified results, including AI connections and repository/release preparation. Use background helpers for concrete independent engineering jobs rather than exposing routine Internal chats to Adrian. Delivery receives their evidence once and keeps the existing work list current; the designated integrator alone controls shared wiring and live activation. Before parking a prior Internal owner, transfer artifacts, unfinished responsibilities and all continuing topics to Delivery. Keep its history intact; archival requires separate explicit human approval. Organizational transfer grants no additional provider, private-data or publication permissions. Review means a result needs human judgment; reuse its existing working conversation unless a sustained new conversation is justified. Saved work means earlier development is retained; it is not an instruction to the person or evidence that the feature is installed.

Keep task completion, human acceptance, chat unread state and chat archival separate. Preserve all continuing topics and artifacts, then ask Adrian before archiving a chat. Keep one existing delivery record and a compact current-state pointer; avoid repeated status copies, recursive acknowledgments and new agents simply to keep chats busy. Use the maintained project-collaboration playbook for the detailed operating process.

## Design the interface around the actual activity

A stage should show the relevant material and the tools needed to work with it. People should see their sources, words, photos and progress rather than encounter generic record placeholders or agent command instructions. Distinguish a recording, its transcripts/corrections, research, authored wording and requested output; connect them visibly without inventing missing material or progress.

Keep the document-first foundation, the typography rule in [PRODUCT](PRODUCT.md#typography), sans-serif controls, project navigation, direct editing and appropriate margin discussion. Preserve each surface's purpose rather than imposing one layout everywhere. Put paths, provenance and technical details behind clear secondary disclosure. Avoid unnecessary top headers, repeated information and horizontal scrolling.

The person must be able to find and resume a story, identify the selected output and see what needs attention. Use human descriptions for empty, processing, failed, read-only and changed-since-review states. Explain what can be done next; do not imply AI ran, sources were verified, work was saved or an output was published without the corresponding evidence.

## Make saved work visibly useful

A chat title alone is not a content library. If the person is invited to interact with saved creative work, present its actual readable document, rendered carousel, image or playable footage with a clear Continue action and known limits. Earlier engineering chats preserve builder history; they are not a list of creations the person should rummage through or mentally reconstruct. Missing previews mean preparation is still needed, not that the person owes feedback. Keep these distinctions even if old sidebar titles remain unchanged.

## Efficiency is part of usability

The person accepts scrolling through useful writing or research. Remove needless top space and repeated notices rather than compressing readable content or hiding it in many boxes. Do not make the author open every quote, slide or transcript to understand the work. Human-first purpose/actions, supported workflow order and actual visible results matter more than explaining the interface in chat. Make guidance controls contextual and meaningful, and show agent-created work without making the person repeat the same inputs.

## Make the document itself readable — adopted 22 October 2026

Research, notes and other working documents serve the person and agents through the same underlying record. Produce a short useful overview followed by semantic headings, descriptive finding leads and one coherent point per paragraph or item, with enough explanation to learn. Keep full analysis available; do not assume only a closing summary will be read. Distinguish evidence, interpretation, uncertainty and possible editorial use, with citations near the claims. Exact provenance belongs in accessible secondary details rather than a default wall of paths. Preserve original wording, attribution, revisions and human choices.

Use a readable text measure, paragraph spacing and meaningful hierarchy on wide and narrow screens. Useful long-form depth may scroll; repeated labels, unnecessary controls and giant unbroken lists should not create the journey. A Library source title or row opens that exact source in its appropriate reader; a redundant Read action adds no useful choice. Presentation and generation guidance both need to meet this intent. Adoption alone does not establish that existing documents, prompts or installed tools have been repaired.

## Let additions earn their place

Evaluate a refinement by whether it makes the author’s actual journey clearer, faster and more trustworthy. Preserve chosen editorial behavior without automatically copying old interface friction. Focused design alternatives resolve consequential open choices; they are not a recurring prerequisite once a direction is chosen. Reliable direct editing, visible creative decisions and actual reachable outputs take priority over adding controls or expanding synchronization. Ordinary use must not become a tour of compulsory workflow steps.

## Check changes through use

Before a changed screen is called usable, open the actual view and ask: Can a returning person tell what this is, what they are looking at, what they can do, and what is still unfinished? Verify the affected action, not just labels, along with required save/switch/recovery behavior. For visual changes inspect the rendered applicable themes and widths; do not ask the person to mentally render code.

When feedback reveals a reusable problem, update these rules or the shared collaboration playbook and relevant brief; preserve their distinct responsibilities rather than duplicating all rules everywhere. Read newer direct human instructions before implementing. This request records rules; it does not authorize an unbounded redesign or claim existing screens have been repaired.

## Interface present is different from connected — 15 October clarification

The author wants the whole intended workflow visible for Claude Design while unfinished connections are built in parallel. Place each already-agreed tool beside its actual work, with its inputs and review path, even if its operation is not connected yet. Clearly explain that state when the person tries it. Do not hide missing tools until implementation finishes or substitute another generic record display. Equally, do not fabricate research, findings, saved edits, delivered outputs or successful actions to fill the design. Keep existing working controls and data intact. A design-ready surface is not a claim of functional completion.

Apply the one typography rule in [PRODUCT](PRODUCT.md#typography) consistently across the long-form views. Keep control labels and compact metadata readable in sans serif and preserve approved platform templates. Arrange offers composition tools; Look presents the audience edition and review/export actions. Checked must explain whether checks have run, which revision they concern, and where the person can work next; an empty screen is not an acceptable interaction.
