Wisdom bank · research · 13 November 2026

Are people building this with AI?

Yes, in pieces. Every piece of your bank exists somewhere as open code or a product. Nobody has put them together, and nobody does the three things that make the bank yours.

The three things nobody does

  • Exact quotes, checked. Tools link an answer back to a chunk of the source. None checks that a quote appears word for word.
  • One idea in several clusters. Every AI clustering tool puts each idea in exactly one group per level. Your "wu wei in tea and in meditation" is missing everywhere.
  • You confirm the clusters. Some tools let a person merge duplicate names. None lets a person approve which cluster an idea belongs to.

So: borrow the engines, build these three yourself. They are small compared with the engines, and they are the heart of the bank.

The bank in five steps

You described it as ingesting, then linking, then the interface for finding, viewing and moving through clusters. Here is what already exists for each step. Tap one to jump.

Step 1

Take in a source

The best example: Heptabase's "Research a topic". You drop in YouTube links, whole playlists or PDFs and press start. It fetches the transcripts itself, lays everything out as cards on a fresh board, and opens a chat beside it whose answers link to the exact paragraph or video timestamp. A good answer becomes a card with one press.

Heptabase's one-button start and timestamp links. Heptabase AI
Reuseengine
CocoIndex has the clearest recipe for YouTube transcript to linked ideas. AR Create's Library already stores YouTube captions with their timings, so this step is mostly done.

Step 2

Distil into concepts, at three depths

RAPTOR is the closest idea to your depths. It groups passages, summarises each group, then summarises the summaries, building a tree from detail to overview. It is built for AI search, not for reading, so the summaries are not written for a person. Borrow the shape and write your three depths for you.

RAPTOR1,780 stars · MIT
Tree of summaries. Last updated September 2024, so take the idea, not the code. RAGFlow (94,000 stars) ships it as a switch. Repo

Its "extract wisdom" prompts are a good start for pulling out every concept.

A few lines of ordinary code: the model returns a quote, the code confirms it is in the source word for word, or rejects it.

Step 3

Link concepts across works

This is what "knowledge graph RAG" projects do, and it is the busiest area of open source right now.

LightRAG40,700 stars · MIT
Most active. Finds ideas and their links across sources, keeps a link back to the source chunk, and has a screen for merging duplicates. Good as the engine that proposes links. Updated 12 Nov 2026. Repo
Graphiti32,800 stars · Apache
The best way of storing it: the source is never changed, every derived fact lists where it came from, and a fact that is later contradicted is closed with a date instead of deleted. Copy this design. Repo
sift-kgsmall
The AI proposes "these are the same", each proposal waits as pending until you approve or decline it, with its reasoning shown. Copy this review screen.

Step 4

Clusters and sub-clusters

Two kinds of method exist. Automatic grouping (Microsoft GraphRAG, BERTopic) builds nested clusters and writes a report for each, but every idea lands in exactly one cluster. AI-written category trees (research such as TnT-LLM and TaxoAdapt) have a model propose named clusters and sub-clusters, and they can place one idea in several. The second fits you.

GraphRAG36,600 stars · MIT
Nested clusters with a written report at every level. Copy the report format for your cluster pages, not the engine. Repo
BERTopic8,000 stars · MIT
Sorts hundreds of thousands of items into a topic tree that you can rename and merge, and accepts topic names you give it. Good for sorting the pile of ideas that have no home yet.
How clusters stay put. Once you confirm a cluster, its name and place are permanent. New ideas are placed into confirmed clusters; nothing gets re-sorted behind your back. Every so often the AI proposes changes as a list you approve or decline.

Step 5

Find, view and move through it

Four interaction patterns worth copying, each from a real product.

Grab, name, askHeptabase
Select some cards, press one key, and they become a named area. Its title stays readable when you zoom far out. You can ask the AI about only that area. One card can sit in any number of boards, and it shows "appears in 4 boards". Docs
The swarmNapkin
One idea sits in the middle and its relatives float around it. Tap one and it becomes the centre. An idea with two tags appears beside both families, so living in several clusters happens naturally. Suggested tags appear and you accept or dismiss them.
Map names by zoomNomic, Embedding Atlas
Far out you see a few broad region names; zoom in and finer names appear, like a geographic map. In Nomic you tap a name to rename it and lasso a group to make a new cluster. Embedding Atlas does the map, but whether it lets you rename regions or make your own clusters is not confirmed. Embedding Atlas
Source beside itElicit, NotebookLM
Tap any claim and the exact passage shows beside it, highlighted in the original. NotebookLM's mind map branches open and close in place.
Still missing everywhere: one surface that has nested clusters, one idea in several clusters, and a zoom from the whole down to a readable concept. Heptabase is closest, and it slows down past about 100 cards on one board.

Underneath

Where it is stored

One database file on your Mac (SQLite) with plain tables: works, exact passages, concepts, links, clusters, which cluster sits inside which (several parents allowed), and which concept is in which cluster with a state of proposed, confirmed or declined. Word search and meaning search live in the same file. A specialised graph database is not needed, because each link is one row with its reason. 300,000 ideas take roughly 1 GB, which a Mac handles easily. Cloudflare can later hold a copy for your phone.

Kuzu, a graph database often recommended for this, was bought by Apple in October 2025 and its code archived, so avoid it.

Next

The order to try it in

  1. Distil one real talk or book you choose with exact quotes checked and three depths. This tests the heart of the bank. It needs you to name the work.
  2. Have the AI propose clusters from it, with one idea allowed in several, and you approve or decline each.
  3. Look at it as a Heptabase-style board and as a map that names regions by zoom, side by side, and pick one.
