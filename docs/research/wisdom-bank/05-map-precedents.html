Maps of ideas that already exist

Ten real examples, with a link to each so you can open them and judge by eye. Findings follow the examples.

I could not open any of these pages from the research session, because its network blocked them. Everything here comes from search results and their summaries. Some links may need a second look, and I have not seen how any of them behaves on a phone.

Examples worth opening

Starts from three simple places (Numbers, Shapes, Change) and opens as you tap. Hand designed.

6. TheBrain

Click a thought and it becomes the centre. Three relations: parent, child and jump (a link between things that neither contains the other).

Borrow
Focus on one thing, few relation kinds, siblings worked out from shared parents.
Avoid
The centre on click detail is from a third party, not TheBrain's page.

7. Kumu

Interactive systems maps. Elements, connections and loops each get a profile page. Connections cannot point at other connections, and there is a help page on the workaround.

Borrow
A page for every connection, and loops as named groups of connections.
Avoid
Some maps are too big for automatic loop detection.

Starts in a view of coloured clusters, then a view of single concepts with their text. It flags structural gaps, pairs of distant clusters with no link.

9. Kinopio

A free canvas of cards. Each line has a type with a name and colour that you can paint onto many lines at once. Types belong to one space only.

Built around a focus question, with words on every line so each link reads as a sentence. Cross-links join different branches and are treated as the creative links.

Patterns that work

  • Open on one thing and its neighbours, with a depth control and filters.
  • Few link kinds, each named and coloured. You already have five.
  • Start small and open on tap, as Quanta does.
  • One axis that carries meaning, such as time, or order along a line.
  • A sentence on every link. It matches your idea that a connection has a reason and your words.
  • Mark the missing step, as InfraNodus does with gaps.

Patterns that fail

  • The whole graph as the front door. Users of Obsidian, Logseq and Roam say it is decoration at size. These are forum posts, not a survey.
  • Links drawn by software from word overlap. They are not your links.
  • Link types or views limited to one board, which split a bank into islands.
  • Hand drawn showpieces. They read well and do not grow without someone redrawing them.

Connections between connections: what exists

  • Wikidata does it in data. A statement is a node of its own that carries qualifiers and references. I found nothing on how that is drawn. Paper page.
  • Kumu says connections can only point to elements, though it has a help page on a workaround I could not read.
  • Kinopio and TheBrain give links a type, and I found no sign a link can itself be linked.
  • Novak maps turn a link into a sentence, which is close in spirit but not a link about a link.

So there are data models for it and no readable picture of it at size. This part of your idea is new ground.

What to test in a mock-up

  • One pathway as a line, phone first. Six to ten stops in order. Where other pathways pass through a stop, show small coloured ticks. Tap a tick to switch lines.
  • The map opens local. Centre on where you came from, depth one, a depth control, a filter by kind, and a highlighted path between two concepts. The whole bank is a button, not the door.
  • Far view as regions with counts, opened on tap, with no lines drawn until you are close.
  • A connection as a small knot you can open. Its page shows kind, reason, your words and any patterns it belongs to. Patterns between connections show as a faint bracket in the local view only.
  • Gaps as dashed stretches on a line that you tap to turn into a research question.

The transit map picture. It fits a pathway very well: ordered, named, few stops, with other lines crossing at shared stops. It strains in three places. A concept in many topics becomes a stop with many lines, so show it as ticks and not as many strokes crossing. Echoes and contrasts have no order, so they are not lines and need another drawing. And the far view cannot be a transit map, because a transit map is only readable with a modest number of stops. That last point comes from how the examples behave, not from a page I read. Use the picture for pathways, and regions plus a local view for everything else.
