## Patterns that work

- **Open on one thing and its neighbours, with a depth control and filters.** Obsidian's local graph, TheBrain's focus, InfraNodus's two levels. All from user reports and docs, not tested by me.
- **Few link kinds, each named and coloured.** Kinopio, TheBrain, Philograph. Adrian already has five.
- **Start small and open on tap**, as Quanta does, instead of drawing everything.
- **One axis that carries meaning**, such as time in Onduygu's map, or order along a line in the metro maps.
- **A sentence on every link** (Novak). It matches Adrian's idea that a connection has a reason and his words.
- **Mark the missing step.** InfraNodus does it with gaps. It matches the "gap in a pathway becomes a question" idea.

## Patterns that fail

- **The whole graph as the front door.** Several users in the Obsidian, Logseq and Roam threads say it is decoration at size. Anecdotal, no survey found.
- **Links drawn by software from word overlap.** They are not the person's links. This repeats the earlier warning about GraphRAG.
- **Types or views scoped to one board**, as in Kinopio, which would split a bank into islands.
- **Hand drawn showpieces** (Quanta, Onduygu) read well but do not grow without a person redrawing.

## Connections between connections: what exists

- **Wikidata** does it in data. A statement is a node of its own that carries qualifiers and references, and the literature calls the result a hyper-relational graph. [Reifying RDF paper page](https://www.korrekt.org/page/Reifying_RDF:_What_Works_Well_With_Wikidata). Source: search summary. I found nothing on how such statements are drawn.
- **Kumu** says connections can only point to elements. It has a FAQ page on a workaround, which I could not read. Its loops are groups of connections with their own profile page.
- **Kinopio and TheBrain** give links a type or kind, but I found no sign that a link can itself be linked.
- **Novak's concept maps** turn a link into a sentence and add cross-links between branches, which is close in spirit but not a link about a link.

So I found data models for it and no readable picture of it at size. That part of Adrian's idea is new ground.

## What to test in a mock-up

1. **One pathway drawn as a line, phone first.** Six to ten stops in order. A stop that other pathways pass through shows small coloured ticks, one per other line. Tap a tick to switch lines.
2. **The map opens local.** Centre on the concept you came from, depth one, a depth control, filter by kind, and a "path between these two concepts" highlight. The whole bank is a button, not the door.
3. **Far view as regions with counts**, opened on tap like Quanta's map, drawing no lines until you are close.
4. **A connection as a small knot you can open.** Its page shows kind, reason, his words and any patterns it belongs to. Patterns between connections are listed on the knot and shown as a faint bracket only in the local view, never as extra lines everywhere.
5. **Gaps as dashed stretches on a line**, which you tap to turn into a research question.

**An honest view of the transit-map picture.** It fits a pathway very well: ordered, named, few stops, with other lines crossing at shared stops. The metro-map research behind it supports the idea that every connection belongs to a line. It will strain in three places. A concept in many topics becomes a stop with many lines, so it must be shown as ticks and not as a crossing of many strokes. Echoes and contrasts are not ordered, so they are not lines and need another drawing. And the far view cannot be a transit map, because a transit map is only readable at a modest number of stops. I take that last point from how the examples behave, not from a source I read. Use the picture for pathways, and use regions and a local view for everything else.
