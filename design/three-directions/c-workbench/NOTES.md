# Direction C — The Workbench

## The idea
AR Create is organised around resuming and the next useful move, across every project at once. The twelve sections are tools you pick up from a rack. They are not stations you walk through, so the app always answers two questions: what was I doing, and what is the smartest thing to do next.

## Opening screen — "The bench"
- **Pick up where you left off.** One large card for the last piece you edited. It shows a real excerpt of the paragraph you were in and a "Since you were here" list with actor labels ("Codex drafted slide 4 of the carousel · not accepted", "Claude checked 'thirty-one years' against the recording"). It has one primary button, **Continue at ¶4**, which opens Write with the caret in that paragraph.
- **Next moves.** One queue of verb sentences drawn from every project ("Correct 4 unclear lines in the harbour recording", "Choose between two angles for Spring Oolong", "Review 6 new photos from Claude's import"). Each one opens the right tool at the right item: the line, slide, file or note is scrolled to and flashed. The queue is worked out from live state, so finishing the work removes the move and lowers the count.
- **Everything active.** Every project on one line with its state ("Correcting the recording · 4 in Source, 2 in Material, 6 in Media"). You can filter it and move through it with `j` / `k` / `/` / Enter.

## How switching works
- **Project switcher** sits at the top left as one line. `⌘K` / `Ctrl-K` opens a finder for projects and tools. Choosing a project scopes everything to it: the rack counts, the next moves, the bench card and the understanding column. Choosing **All projects** brings the cross-project view back.
- **Tool rack** on the left lists the twelve tools, grouped Gather / Develop / Make / Deliver. Each tool shows an honest count of what needs you and shows nothing when nothing does. `[` collapses the rack to icons.
- When the scope is **All**, a tool that works on one piece shows a quiet "In:" strip listing the projects that have material for it, each with its count. Material and Media show everything at once, tagged by project.
- **Understanding column** on the right (`]` toggles it) follows the project you are working in. It holds the current understanding, **Your thoughts — Adrian** (a box that only he writes in, plus a quick "Add a thought" from anywhere with `T`), decisions, protected phrases, corrections and rejected angles, each labelled with where it applies.
- **Phone (390px):** the rack becomes a bottom bar (Bench + the four groups). Tapping a group opens a small sheet of its tools. The understanding column becomes a bottom sheet.

## What it is best at
- Coming back after a gap, or juggling six or more pieces at once. The bench costs no orientation and needs no manual.
- Agent work appears where it matters, with the actor named, without asking Adrian to import anything or answer again.
- Carry-forward is visible. Correcting a Source line adds a dated correction to the column. Choosing an angle records a decision and moves the other angle into Rejected angles. Protected phrases are underlined in Write and counted in Check.
- One origin with several outputs is explicit in Outputs: the origin first, then a branch for each output, each with its own state and its own tool.

## What it costs (tradeoffs)
- **It suggests rather than guides.** Nothing tells a newcomer the "right order". That suits Adrian but gives a beginner less scaffolding.
- **Next moves need trustworthy counts.** If a count is wrong or stale, the bench lies. Every count needs a real, cheap query behind it, and agents have to write actor and time on every change.
- **Three columns are dense.** The rack, the work and the understanding column all compete at 1280px. Focus mode and the collapsible rack and column carry the load, and Write at 1100px narrows the margin.
- **The "In:" strip under All** adds a step before a single-piece tool. Pinning a project removes it.
- **Ranking is hand-ordered here.** A real product would need a rule for ordering next moves (age, blocking, agent-made first?), and getting that rule wrong is the main risk.

## What building it on the current shell needs
The current shell is a left sidebar with 12 steps, a work column and a Margin/Decisions inspector. It maps closely:
1. **Sidebar → rack.** Keep the 12 entries but drop the step numbering as an implied order. Add the Gather/Develop/Make/Deliver group labels, an icon-only collapsed state and a per-tool "needs you" count. Each count needs a small count endpoint (or one aggregate call) per project and for all projects together.
2. **New home route, "The bench".** It needs a "last edited position" per piece (piece id + paragraph/slide id + time), a changes-since feed of actor-labelled events (an audit log filtered by `actor != you` since your last edit), and a next-moves query built from the same counts.
3. **Project scope and finder.** A global scope in the shell state (`all | projectId`) that every tool reads, plus a `⌘K` finder over projects and tools. Tools that need one piece need the "In:" chooser when the scope is All.
4. **Inspector → understanding column.** Extend Margin/Decisions to cover current understanding, Adrian's own thoughts (stored as his authored record and never mixed with agent text), protected phrases, corrections and rejected angles, each with a "where it applies" field. Margin notes stay inside Write as the editorial margin. Focus hides the rack, the column and the margin.
5. **Deep links into items.** Each tool must accept a target (`line`, `slide`, `paragraph`, `asset`, `material item`) so a next move lands on the exact thing.
6. **Still not connected (shown honestly in the prototype):** transcription, recording playback, bilingual Source, broad research/retrieval, AI discussion of a point, AI editor help, running a Check, Material keep/remove review, sharing links, export. Every Save says "Prototype — nothing is saved".

## Revision 2 (Adrian's feedback)
Adrian picked the Workbench. His notes: too many columns and panels, too many different sizes, Read and Write should match, every page had its own type rules, he disliked one-click destination pages like Check, and he asked "how do we pull out from the side". He wanted something smaller and easier to read, with a button to make it bigger. The reference is the comparison page: Plus Jakarta Sans, body 15/400, headings 15/600, quiet labels 13, no display headings.

This revision supersedes the "Understanding column" and "Three columns are dense" lines above.

- **One type system.** Five tokens at the top of the file: `--t-label` 13px, `--t-body` 15px, `--t-reading` 16px/1.65 (Read and Write prose), `--t-head` 15px/600, `--t-title` 17px/600. Weights are only 400, 500 and 600. Every other size and weight was removed, including the 24–34px display titles, uppercase letter-spaced labels and the 11–12.5px in-between sizes. Browser defaults for `h1`–`h4`, `b`, `small` and `kbd` are reset to tokens. A computed-style audit across all 19 screens and drawers finds only the token values.
- **Read and Write are the same document.** They share the same column (`--measure`, 40rem), the same font, the same 16px size, the same 1.65 line height and the same left edge. Read is the Write page with the margin and editing removed.
- **Serif only for speech.** Lora appears only on what people said: Source transcript lines and Research evidence quotes, at body size.
- **Same left edge, same top.** Every screen, the bench included, starts with one title line at the same padding. "Back to ¶n in Write" now sits in that title line instead of a row above it.
- **Text size control.** A− / A+ in the top line goes from 87.5% to 137.5% in 12.5% steps. It sets one root multiplier, so every token scales together. The setting is remembered in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch).
- **Two columns at most.** The rack (slim, icon + label, `[` collapses it to icons) and the work. The permanent right "understanding" column is gone. The bench, Research (the open point expands in place) and Audience are now single columns too.
- **One side drawer.** It is about 380px wide, pulls out from the right over the work, closes with Esc, and leaves the work visible behind it. It holds Understanding (current understanding, Your thoughts, decisions, protected phrases, corrections, rejected angles), Check, History and Media "used in". Open it from the Understanding button in the top line, `]`, `T` (add a thought), the small pull tab on the right edge, or the Check / History buttons in Write and Read. Tabs inside switch between Understanding, Check and History. On a phone it takes the full width.
- **Check is a pull-out, not a page.** The Check entry in the rack is marked as a pull-out. From another screen it opens Write and pulls Check out beside it. It keeps the honest "No check has run on this revision" state with Run a check (not connected). Below that are **local text checks**, labelled "counted in this page, not a check": protected phrases present or missing, corrected wording present, and unsure or disputed research points the text leans on. Clicking one highlights its passage in Write or Read. Rejected angles and tone are stated as needing the real check.
- **Write's margin** stays inside the work column as the document's margin. Focus hides it, along with the rack and the drawer.
- **Media "Used in N places"** pulls out the drawer with the asset and each place, each with an Open link.
- **Real stories.** The "Pick up where you left off" card and every project row on the bench have an "Open in AR Create" link to `http://127.0.0.1:8841/document-study/index.html` (new tab, `rel="noopener"`). The note "Opens your installed app at its home; choose the story there — direct story links aren't built yet" is shown once on the card and once above the list, and is the link's title on every row. All story text in the prototype is still invented sample text. Nothing is fetched.
- **Numbers dropped** from the rack, the titles and the finder (01–12 implied an order).

**Still open:** the bench card's "Since you were here" and the History drawer show the same invented events. In a real build both should read one actor-labelled change feed.

## Revision 3 — scale, research, templates
Adrian's note: the prototypes could not hold many articles, the research had no terminal, and the carousel templates he built were missing. Course corrections during the build: organise with **nested folders**, keep every list item to **one line (two at most)**, keep the drawer for **one item or a tool, never for browsing**, and use a realistic sample (not thousands by default) while proving the design holds thousands.

Code: `scale.js` and `scale.css` extend the revision-2 page through `window.ARC`. Revision 2's rules are unchanged: the five type tokens, weights 400/500/600, A−/A+, two columns at most, one right drawer.

- **Library** (top of Gather; Material stays the intake inbox and links to "Library → Unsorted"). 214 invented sources (articles with publication, YouTube with channel and duration, recordings, PDFs/books, notes, photo sets), seeded so they are the same on every load. Inside the work column: a collapsible **folder tree** (folders inside folders to any depth, counts include subfolders, "All sources" and "Unsorted" at the top), a breadcrumb that shows a collection's question, search, facet selects with counts (type, topic, status, used/unused), sort, and saved views (All, Unsorted, YouTube not yet watched, Used in Spring Oolong, To read later). The table is **virtualised**: only the visible rows exist (about 20). "N shown of M sources" is always visible. You can select with checkboxes, shift-click a range, or select all matching. The bulk bar offers add to collection or folder, add to piece, tag topic and mark read. You can drag rows onto a folder (an item can sit in several folders), drag a folder onto another to nest it, and add a new folder inside the current one. Clicking a row opens one item's preview in the drawer: an excerpt, a video thumbnail with duration and chapters, or a transcript snippet. All of it is labelled Sample. The preview also has "Use in piece…", "Add to collection…" and the folders it sits in (removable). A hidden "Stress test: load 5,000 more" link at the foot of the tree (or `?stress=1`) proves the scale.
- **Collections** are folders that carry a question, in the same tree. The screen lists each one on one line (name, path, count) with its question as a second line, plus an "Unsorted" bucket. **Suggest sorting** opens a review of proposed assignments grouped by collection, with a match strength. You can accept or reject a whole group or one item. It is labelled "Sample suggestions — sorting isn't connected yet": the proposals come from topic-word matching in the page, not from Claude. Accepting does move items, locally only.
- **Pieces** (top of Make): all 31 pieces across projects, in the same folder tree and one-line table, with Kind, Status and Project facets, saved views (Drafts, Ready to ship, Instagram ideas) and bulk folder and status changes. The sample projects' pieces open in Write or Arrange. The others say plainly that they are list-only here.
- **Write → Sources** tab in the drawer: the piece's linked collection, what is cited, and what sits in the collection but is not cited, one line each. Dragging a source into the text inserts a citation chip, stored as a token. **Add sources** opens a picker that is the same virtualised table, starting in the piece's collection.
- **Research** = the points list and a **research console**, split inside the work column. The console has a `research ›` prompt, command hints and ↑/↓ history. `/search` runs a real local search over the Library in the page. `/collection`, `/point n`, `/cite <id>` and `/compare <id> <id>` all work on local data. Any plain question prints "Claude isn't connected here yet — your question is saved to this collection's research log. Local search found N items:" followed by local results. Nothing is generated. Points use Sure / Unsure / Disputed (the old "likely" points became Unsure, so the Research count went up) and each has "Discuss in console", which fills in `/point n`.
- **Templates in Arrange.** The page loads Adrian's `ig-layouts.json` (58 layouts, 6 groups). If the file is opened directly it shows an honest fallback that says to serve the folder. The carousel shows as a row of real 1080×1350 renders scaled to about 200px, each with its template name. You edit the text in place under each slide, and the render updates as you type. Drag the grip to reorder. Clicking a slide's template name opens the picker: all 58 as live mini renders, filtered by group, role (cover/hook/inner/close) and photo count. Clicking one applies it. Photos are invented SVG gradients marked "Sample photo", and the logo is a plain SVG wordmark. The template fonts (EB Garamond, Caudex, Shippori Mincho, Noto Serif TC, Source Serif 4, Instrument Sans) are loaded only for the renders. Arrange also has **Post** (porc, single 1080×1350) and **Product listing** (oolong listing image). Articles say they use the magazine layout, and scripts say no template system exists yet.
- **Finder** lists pinned and recent projects first.
- **Phone (390px):** the table becomes two-line rows, and the folders, facets and saved views move into a "Folders and filters" sheet. The research split stacks, and the carousel row scrolls inside itself. Nothing scrolls sideways.

**Still not connected:** AI sorting, Claude in the console (questions are only kept), opening an original source, real thumbnails and chapters, saving anything, export of rendered templates, and Media at 2,000 photos (Media is still the revision-2 grid). Bench next moves are not yet grouped and capped, and drawer lists are not yet searchable.

## Revision 4 — Media at scale, Arrange picker
Adrian's note: "Give Media the same folder tree and one-line list, sized for 2,000 photos, and reuse it as the photo picker in Arrange." His standing rules still hold: folders nest to any depth, you can drag items and folders, an item can be linked into several folders, counts include subfolders, and there is a breadcrumb. Rows stay one line (two at most). The drawer only previews one item or holds a tool. The type scale (13 / 15 / 16 / 15-600 / 17-600, weights 400/500/600) and the two-column maximum are unchanged.

This supersedes revision 3's "Media at 2,000 photos (Media is still the revision-2 grid)".

Code: `media.js` and `media.css`. Media is not a second implementation. It is one more domain in revision 3's browser engine: the folder tree, virtualised one-line table, facets, saved views, multi-select, bulk bar, drag-to-folder and phone sheet. To make that possible, `scale.js` now exposes the engine through `window.ARC_SCALE` with small hooks: tree labels, bulk buttons, column sort keys and sort getters, per-key options, an `onPick` row handler, and a virtualised **grid mode** that any domain gets by defining `tile()`. Library, Pieces and the Add-sources picker behave as before.

- **Media.** 278 invented photos and videos, the same on every load, in a nested structure:
  - Trips › Wuyi 2026 › Day 1 / Day 2 / Day 3, Trips › Alishan 2025 › Gardens / Tea houses, and Trips › Ipoh 2026
  - Studio › Teaware shoot / Kiln Day › Firing / Opening the kiln / Behind the scenes
  - Products › Spring Oolong / Teaware › Gaiwans / Yixing pots / Dark tea
  - Projects › Porcelain and Tea / Harbour Night Market
  - Posted to Instagram › 2025 / 2026
  - Unsorted

  Some items are linked into two folders. Counts include subfolders and count a linked item once: Trips 132, Wuyi 2026 82 (22 + 34 + 26), Kiln Day 36. Revision 2's assets keep their ids, so the bench's "Review 6 new photos", Write ¶3 and the carousel still point at them.
- **Each row is one line:** a small square gradient thumbnail (play mark on video), title, file name, kind (Photo or "Video · 0:12"), date, folder (+N when it is linked into more than one), and "Used in N". On a phone the row becomes two lines. A **List / Grid** toggle switches to a compact thumbnail grid. The grid is virtualised by tile-row, so only the visible tiles exist.
- **Facets:** Kind, Folder (top level), Date (last 30 days, earlier in 2026, 2025, 2024 and before), Used, Orientation, Project, Added by. **Saved views:** All, Unused, Videos, From Claude's import. The header button "6 new from Claude's import" opens that view.
- **Bulk:** Link to folder (keeps the other folders), Move to folder (replaces them), Tag, Add to piece. Rows and tiles drag onto folders, and folders drag onto folders.
- **The drawer previews one item:** a larger image (an invented SVG with the orientation's real proportions), the file, kind, date, place, size, who added it and the project. Then come **Used in N places**, one line each with Open (Open goes to the exact slide in Arrange, or to the paragraph in Write), and the folders it sits in, each removable. New imports get Keep / Set aside here.
- **"Used in" is worked out, not stored twice.** Carousel slides, the post and the listing image each hold their photo ids per slot. A photo's uses are its stored uses (Write, pieces, posted) plus every slot that currently shows it. Reordering slides renumbers its uses, and changing a template to one with fewer photos drops the slots it no longer has.
- **Arrange picker.** Under each slide's render there is a one-line slot strip: a thumbnail per photo slot, then the photo's title, or "Choose photos · 1 of 2" for multi-photo templates. Clicking a slot, the strip link or the render itself opens **the same Media browser as a sheet over the work area**. The rack stays visible, and it is not a third column. The sheet starts in the project's folder (Porcelain and Tea → Projects › Porcelain and Tea), with Photo pre-filtered and the grid as the default. Search, the tree and every facet work as in Media. Clicking a photo places it: the template render's `__IG_PHOTO_n__` / `__IG_BG_n__` is replaced with that photo, the toast says "now used in N places", and Media shows the new use. For multi-photo templates there are slot tabs ("Photo 1", "Photo 2 · empty"). A click fills the current slot and moves to the next one. Selecting several photos and pressing **Fill N slots in order** fills them from the current slot onward. Videos are refused for a photo slot. The post and the product listing image work the same way.
- **Stress test:** the hidden "Stress test: load 2,000 more" link at the foot of the tree (or `?mstress=1`) adds 2,000 older items into a nested Archive › year › quarter branch, so the realistic folders stay realistic.

## Revision 5 — rail and section panel
Adrian's note on Library: four columns fought (rack, a boxed folder tree, the list, a pop-out preview), titles were crushed to "Posting rhy…", the preview covered the list, and helper text and the stress link sat where folders should be. He asked for a Spotify-style sidebar: a compact rail, and a second panel that comes out of the selected item only when it is needed, so the work gets the room.

This supersedes revision 2's "two columns at most" (now: rail + panel + work, the panel only where there is something to navigate) and revision 3/4's in-list folder tree and preview drawer for browsing. The type scale (13 / 15 / 16 / 15-600 / 17-600, weights 400/500/600, A−/A+), the shared browser engine (virtualised list, facets, multi-select, bulk bar, drag-to-folder), templates, the research console and the honesty labels are unchanged.

- **Rail** (66px): icon, a small 13px label and a count badge. The bench is at the top. Gather / Develop / Make / Deliver are thin dividers with no heading text. The active item has a lifted background and a sand bar. Clicking the active item again opens or closes its panel; `[` does the same.
- **Material and Collections are no longer rail items.** Material is the Library's **Inbox** (first row of the Library panel, carries Material's count on the Library badge). Collections are folders with a question: a small "?" in the tree and the question shown under the title line of that folder's list. The Collections screen and Suggest sorting stay reachable from Unsorted ("Suggest sorting" in its title line).
- **Section panel** (240px, same background as the rail, no border box), only for: Library (Inbox, All sources, Unsorted, then the nested tree), Media (photo folders), Pieces (pieces by project folder), Source (recordings, plus recordings not yet transcribed that sit in the Inbox), Research (collections; the current one shows its points), Outputs (outputs grouped by kind).
