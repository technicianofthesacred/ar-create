Three ways AR Create could work next

Three working prototypes, built on the approved C3 look and the same invented tea project, so you compare structure, not content. Each one walks the three journeys you asked for and shows the empty, unsaved, processing, failed, stale and read-only states. Option B also holds one real story, the published Porcelain and Tea, read-only.

How to use: press Play on an option, then use the gold bar at the top. “Walk through” plays a journey step by step; “Show state” switches any screen into an awkward state. Nothing in them saves, generates, sends or exports. Your installed app on port 8841 was not touched.

The three directions

A · The Page — document-first

One long page per checkpoint, centred like a good document. All twelve checkpoints sit in one line across the top in four groups. Notes, findings and history sit in the margin. Library slides in from the right without leaving the page.

Strong at
  • Content dominates; scrolling reads like a document
  • Closest to today’s C3, so the least to rebuild
  • Writing, Research and Source feel the same: calm pages
Gives up
  • Least sense of the whole project at a glance
  • Connections are described in words, not shown
  • The top bar is long; on a phone it wraps to two lines

B · The Studio — project-first Recommended frame

A sidebar holds Home, Library, Inquiries and Projects with their pieces. Each project opens on your thoughts and a map: Origins → Understanding → Direction → Pieces. Click a card and everything it feeds lights up. Checkpoints split by owner: the project owns Material to Direction and Outputs; each piece owns Write to Share.

Strong at
  • Shows the connections you asked for, honestly and at a glance
  • The sidebar makes switching pieces and returning one click
  • Splitting checkpoints by owner matches how the data really works
Gives up
  • One more level to understand (project vs piece tabs)
  • The map needs real “which passage feeds which piece” links; today only some exist
  • The sidebar takes width from writing (it can collapse)

C · The Bench — creative workbench

Everything at once. The Shelf on the left (Library, Material, Media), the work on the bench in the middle, the Thread on the right (decisions, findings, history, “where used”), and every piece as a tile in the tray at the bottom. A “Go to anything” palette reaches all twelve checkpoints.

Strong at
  • Library is truly on demand: always one glance away
  • “Where used” answers two-way questions instantly
  • Concept as a board of cards you drag into order
Gives up
  • Busiest screen; three panes compete with the writing
  • On a phone the panes stack, so it becomes a long scroll
  • Most new building: almost nothing in today’s layout carries over

Recommendation

Build B’s frame, with A’s pages inside it, and borrow two things from C. B solves the hardest problems in your brief: knowing where you are, seeing how a correction or a photo connects, and keeping pieces independent. A’s calm document pages are the best place to actually read and write, so Write, Read, Check, Source and Research keep A’s page-and-margin layout inside B. From C, take the “Where used” panel (two-way connections for any passage, photo or source) and the always-available Library shelf, as a collapsible side panel rather than a permanent third column.

Keep from today

Tabs for open pieces, the four checkpoint groups, Margin / Decisions / Readiness beside Write, the bottom bar with save status and A− / A+, the Waiting-on-you band, Work receipts, Undo, and the real exports. They all appear in the prototypes.

Safest first step

A’s top bar and margin can go into the current app with the least risk, because they decorate today’s screens like C3 does. B’s sidebar and project map come next, since they need the prepared Collections and connections installed.

What I decided for you

Dark by default, the C3 palette, no sideways scrolling anywhere (bars wrap), drag and drop for reordering slides and cards, plain labels instead of status codes, and every not-yet-connected tool says so where you hit it.

About the Magazine workflow

I read the Magazine repository. Its fifteen steps map onto the twelve checkpoints, and four decisions that are yours today show in every option: your own entry and closing lines stay in your words, connecting lines wait for your yes, every quote traces back to the transcript, and photos come last, placed between thoughts. The real Porcelain and Tea story in Option B shows how that markup reads inside the new layout.

Not covered by any option yet: blind fresh-reader reviews and the rating loop, which have no home in AR Create so far. They could sit in Check.

Your call

Pick one, or name a combination (for example “B with A’s pages and C’s Where used”). Then the build can start on a branch, beginning with the pieces that are already working.
