## 2026-11-16 — The wisdom bank: what goes in, how it reads, how it links

**Topic:** Over 13 to 16 November Adrian brought new ideas to the wisdom bank: clusters of ideas that overlap (one idea in many groups), whether people already build this with AI, and how a talk should be broken down and written. A real talk (Joe Hudson on helping as fear) was distilled four times as a test. At stake: defining the unit of the bank and its writing before anything is captured, because everything later depends on it.

**Convergent answer:** The bank is Adrian's own school and writer's guide: a living web of concepts from teachings, ancient texts, trainings and neuroscience, connected like pathways, that he studies, researches, is trained by and writes from, aiming at connections nobody has made. Taking in a source prepares clean signal and never digests it: each idea is written so it stands on its own in natural English, keeps the source's meaning exactly (clarity allowed, reinterpretation not), and carries its exact words. Each level only adds to the one before, opened by a real story or exact line from the source. Concepts are taken in at their surface, their essence, and their elements. Cross-linking runs on one shared index of controlled tags, meaning fingerprints, the same named things, and two-step paths; AI judges only a shortlist, and nothing counts until Adrian confirms. Grouping happens two ways: the bank proposes, and Adrian suggests links that the bank then tests with a bridge report. The deeper point: reading as a person comes first; the connecting machinery stays behind the scenes and never bends the text.

**Rejected paths, with reasons:**
- Labelled sections inside a reading (what it is, what you do, how you embody it, misunderstood): too many classifiers, too big to take in.
- Compressed one-line distillations and abstract essences: only make sense after reading the source; "English literary, not AI".
- First-person "we" voice: it only needs to stand on its own.
- "He says" reporting: everything is sourced, so state the idea.
- Adding teaching questions, argument shapes or study structure at taking in: the program is the connector, not the digester; Adrian shapes and questions the signal himself.
- Bringing other sources or advice into a source's own reading: filters it through what Adrian wants to hear; that is a separate, marked layer, only when asked.
- A cliff note that retells the gem: each level must add.
- Free tags: they drift into synonyms that never meet.
- A specialised graph database: links are rows with a reason; one database file is enough.
- AI comparing everything with everything: impossible at size; cheap steps first, AI on a shortlist.
- Proposing a single test source as the next move when he asked for the system: he is building the whole system.

**The honest tensions left unresolved:**
- Whether the five kinds of unit (concept, claim, practice, example, open question) are right; he has not confirmed.
- The starting index of themes, fields and scales does not exist yet.
- Faithfulness versus readability will keep pulling; the line is "clarity allowed, meaning unchanged", tested one example at a time.
- The interface is still undecided; Embedding Atlas was the leading base before this, and none of these pages tested it.
- Nothing has been tried across two different sources, so linking is designed but unproven.
